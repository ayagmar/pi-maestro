# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

The site serves three equally important audiences: existing Pi users looking for dependable multi-agent orchestration, developers evaluating Pi extensions and agent workflows, and engineering teams exploring lower-cost autonomous coding execution. They need to understand how Pi Maestro works, judge whether its execution and review model is trustworthy, install it, and find the operational or technical documentation relevant to them.

## Product Purpose

Pi Maestro is a Pi extension that adds an orchestrator/advisor and executor workflow. It turns a goal into bounded tasks, delegates them to fresh-context executors, independently reviews their results, and keeps execution, cost, state, evidence, and recovery visible.

The Astro site exists to explain and demonstrate that workflow, showcase Pi Maestro's real capabilities, and provide complete user, architecture, configuration, operations, security, and contributor documentation. Success means visitors can quickly understand the model, see credible evidence of it working, install the extension, and confidently operate it.

## Positioning

Pi Maestro gives Pi an orchestrator/advisor layer: it breaks goals into work, sends tasks to fresh-context executors, and brings the results back for independent review and approval.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary and secondary CTA: “Install Pi Maestro” is primary; “Read the docs” is secondary. “See it in action” supports both when demo media is available.
- The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds: An orchestrator/advisor for Pi, backed by fresh-context executors and independent review.
- Belief ladder: Pi is intentionally minimal and extensible; Pi Maestro adds a concrete orchestrator/executor pattern without replacing that foundation; fresh-context execution keeps delegated work focused; independent review and visible evidence make the workflow dependable; installation and operation are documented clearly enough to adopt with confidence.
- Proof on hand: the working extension and test suite under `src/` and `test/`; product detail in `README.md`; architecture evidence in `docs/architecture.md`; configuration evidence in `docs/configuration.md`; operational and recovery guidance in `docs/operations.md`; and security policy in `SECURITY.md`. Screenshots and short workflow demos will be added later under `.impeccable/assets/proof/` and should be presented as optional, performance-conscious evidence rather than required background media.

## Brand Personality

Dependable, technically sharp, and transparent. The experience should create confidence and control: visitors should feel that Pi Maestro makes multi-agent execution observable rather than chaotic. The voice should be direct and technically credible, explaining concrete behavior instead of relying on hype.

## Anti-references

Avoid generic AI marketing: vague transformation claims, interchangeable SaaS language, decorative AI gradients, fake productivity promises, unexplained abstractions, and visuals that imply capability without showing evidence. Avoid turning the site into a dense terminal-only presentation; terminal artifacts should demonstrate the product while the surrounding content remains clear and approachable.

## Design Principles

1. **Show the system working.** Use real workflows, product states, architecture, and evidence instead of abstract AI claims.
2. **Make orchestration legible.** Explain the advisor, executor, reviewer, and human roles so the workflow feels controlled rather than magical.
3. **Earn trust through transparency.** Surface boundaries, costs, review behavior, recovery paths, and security decisions alongside benefits.
4. **Treat documentation as part of the product.** User guides and technical references should be easy to navigate, scan, and connect back to real tasks.
5. **Use media as proof, not decoration.** Screenshots and short demos should clarify behavior, load on demand, and never make the site heavy or incomplete without them.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Support complete keyboard navigation, strong text and control contrast, readable prose and code examples, visible focus states, reduced-motion preferences, and status communication that does not rely on color alone. Demo media should include useful poster images, captions or equivalent text, and user-controlled playback.
